Carbon materials are ubiquitous, playing a role in everyday tasks such as writing with graphite and special moments such as a marriage proposal with a diamond. Carbons are central to everyday life and also find applications in modern-day engineering, offering ways to remove pollutants from water, transform chemical feedstocks into more valuable products, or store charge in batteries or capacitors.

Despite their long history and widespread technological use, much remains to be discovered. Modern-day carbon materials are part of the world of nanotechnology, including remarkable materials such as graphene or carbon nanotubes, whose expensive synthesis limits their commercial adoption despite their breathtaking properties. Yet, graphene should not be the only material in the spotlight: doped carbons and porous carbons are underdog heroes that allow many technologies to be developed cost-effectively.

Part of my research was based on such materials. I have shown how to obtain [highly oxidation-resistant materials](https://pubs.acs.org/aamick/article/13/25/29612/369311/Caffeine-Derived-Noble-Carbons-as-Ball-Milling), making it easier to process electrode materials for capacitors. I have shown how such oxidation resistance can be used to generate [new catalysts with unique properties](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2211285522002725?via%3Dihub) for electrochemical conversions. Finally, I have shown how much heteroatom content can change the properties of these materials when tuned to [unprecedented loadings](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/anie.202211663).
