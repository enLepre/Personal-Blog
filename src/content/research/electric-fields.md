Chemistry is the world of electrical forces. Atoms are held together by the Coulomb attraction between nuclei and electrons. This attraction is immense, reaching values greater than 10 GV/m. For reference, thunder occurs when a 10 MV/m field is present between a cloud and the ground. Chemistry happens at energy scales thousands of times larger than those of one of the most impressive natural phenomena observed by humans.

However, this immense number is its own judgment. Because physics permits such conditions only inside atoms, applying a similar field to a macroscopic system is nearly impossible. For this reason, chemistry exists. It is the subject that studies how to use the fields of the nuclei themselves to arrange those nuclei in new ways. That's the magic that I have never seen described in chemistry.

Yet, there are places where those forces can be observed. Enzymes can have similar fields in their pockets, and that's nature's secret to making everything we see and find fascinating. Electrical double layers can also generate fields of almost the same magnitude. Yet, they still struggle to reach the same magnitude as those in enzymes. That's my field of research. I want to better understand how these fields behave at the interfaces between materials and electrolytes, to harvest their potential energy and carry out reactions that only enzymes were previously able to perform.
