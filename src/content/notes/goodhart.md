Organizations of people serve as a tool to focus individual efforts towards problems too large to be addressed by single persons. How to focus these efforts is not a trivial problem and part of those individuals specializes on directing other people tasks in the best direction to solve the problem. Among the challenges, evaluating whether the problem is being solved or not is the central obstacle, making management a non-trivial task. When problems are particularly long, evaluations end up considering sections rather the whole picture, and individuals tend to optimize for these smaller sections rather than the bigger picture.

> when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.

That's [Goodhart's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law) describing how we fail to evaluate performance of individuals or organization when we rely on metrics. It is a hurtful concept when you think quantification is the key to solving problem because it describes where it fails. Yet, looking into the development of academic metrics it becomes very clear how it works. For example, focusing only on h-index or impact factor makes research less interested in long term research, preferring short incremental projects with more smaller papers as output. Avoiding long term research makes professionals less aware of bigger pictures and training of future researchers less aware of long term goals. Hence, scientific output becomes less relevant and society loses technological advancements, and their economic benefit. 

I feel the problem as very similar to quantum mechanic where one tries to measure the position and the speed of an electron. Indeed, to know the speed of an object one need to know its position at two different times. However, to know this position we use  the interaction of the object with light. This interaction is negligible for macroscopic objects but it is not for quantum particles like electrons. Therefore, when measuring the position of one electron with light we are simultaneously changing its state, impeding the measurement via classical methods. Therefore, quantum mechanics relies on probability rather than precise values. Similarly, humans respond to incentives and evaluations, optimizing for what is better seen and neglecting what is not.
