I am an avid user of YouTube, which has basically become my source of information like a personalized magazine. Among the content I consume, one format that has attracted my attention is the [*... but I respect your time*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=foO1QakhjZE&pp=ygUXYnV0IGkgcmVzcGVjdCB5b3VyIHRpbWU%3D) where a review of a product is provided in 2-3 minutes instead of the traditional 20–30-minute video essay. *This meeting could have been an email* feels the same way, expressing the frustration when information is not delivered efficiently. I experience the same feeling when interacting with a large language model, trying to understand hard concepts from scattered literature, becoming worse and worse when the same slop is published freely on LinkedIn. I open Substack trying to find higher quality information, but I end up scrolling through endless essays saying the same thing over and over. I read this same text, finding it difficult to not feel the same frustration and I ask myself: *what is the point of all of this?* 

Writing has never been my strongest skill. I really got into it only during my PhD because writing a scientific argument felt like water flowing in the mountains. You know where you are starting and where you should finish, and you are building the riverbed, guiding your audience through your train of thought. The more conscious I became of this process, the more critically I began to read. The strict constraints of scientific writing leave little room for rhetorical devices and force me to think deeply about every sentence I construct: *Is it true? Who is saying the opposite? Why are they saying the opposite?* I have started to ask the same questions about the texts I read as well, helping me understand more about the writer than about the topic. The same inefficiencies I complained about earlier allow for a personal connection, even if the personal connection is anger. *Where is the limit then?*

Length matters. When I scroll through an essay and the scrollbar is the size of my thumb I get discouraged. I opened that page intending to learn about the hydrogen economy. Why am I reading about the Roman Empire? Ok, let me open (ex-)twitter and scroll through the endless list of slop. At least I can quickly give up on something when it does not match my interests. A complex argument needs space, but how can I commit to it if it does not assure me a return on my investment? These are often my thoughts when I try to commit to a long text, and I understand completely why (ex-)twitter wins over a book. I do look for information, but **I  prefer a long list of ideas in competition rather than a long list of reasons why one single idea is correct**. 

I want my writing to follow this philosophy. My guess is that a four-paragraph structure is a middle ground between short- and long-form writing. I'll try to construct my notes with an introduction (or hook), two body paragraphs, and a conclusion. Nothing more. Nothing less. This is the style I want to embrace in my notes as much as in my scientific endeavours. It is not easy, but it is my best way to respect our time.
