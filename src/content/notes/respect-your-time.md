I am an avid user of Youtube, which has basically become my source of information like a personalized magazine. Among the content I consume, one format that has attracted my attention is the [*... but I respect your time*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=foO1QakhjZE&pp=ygUXYnV0IGkgcmVzcGVjdCB5b3VyIHRpbWU%3D) where a review of a product is provided in 2-3 minutes instead of the traditional 20-30 minutes video essay. *This meeting could have been an email* feels the same way, expressing the frustration when information are not delivered efficiently. I experience the same feeling when interacting with a Large Language Model, trying to understand hard concepts from a scattered literature, becoming worse and worse when the same slop is published freely on LinkedIn. I open Substack trying to find higher quality information, but I end up scrolling through endless essays saying over and over the same thing. I read this test and I find it difficult to not feel the same frustration and I ask myself: *what is the point of all of this?* 

Personal branding is becoming more and more important to get recognition, but what does personal branding means for someone in academia? Having a post a day on LinkedIn? Replying angrily to the n-case of scientific misconduct? Provide a fake support comment or rigid critique on a collegue post?
