It is very difficult to judge the quality of work in scientific research. While there are certain red flags you can consider, their significance may change when you take the researcher's background into account. For a physicist with little training in solution preparation, preparing a solution might be a big challenge, whereas for a chemist trained in the technique it might not be. A chemist watching that physicist prepare a solution might be quick to conclude that the work is of poor quality, but that judgment would overlook differences in training.

Judgment also hangs like the sword of Damocles over everyone competing for a position, and it can be used to gain an advantage over peers in increasingly fierce competition. It is only natural that human beings responding to incentives would criticise colleagues to take advantage of their weaknesses. However, in doing so, they lose sight of what research means.

Research means investigating unknown avenues where unexpected consequences will arise. More often than expected, those consequences appear obvious only after the damage is done. Yet, the same consequences may seem impossible beforehand. For this reason, experimenting and making mistakes are fundamental to uncovering deeper truths about our universe. The mistakes that help us learn are those that reveal something unexpected despite careful work; they should be distinguished from errors caused by avoidable negligence.

Using judgments about other people's mistakes as a weapon to establish a hierarchy goes against the point of this process. It makes researchers less willing to take risks and increases their fear of designing new experiments. I think this is a battle we need to fight together, to allow everyone, from bachelor's students to professors, room for imperfection. That space can provide everyone with important new information that would otherwise be lost.

So, next time you see an obvious mistake, don't judge. Think about the background, ask if help is needed, and be ready to offer support when the mistake is uncovered. The aim is to grow together because, in the end, if you are not making mistakes, you are not working on hard enough problems.
