I did my phd in materials science from 2019 to 2023. I remeber when chatGPT was announced. I remember trying after fa ew weeks everyone was hyping about it, finding no utility, and dropping it to focus on my real research. During the same time, my biggest struggle was literature review. I worked on carbon materials for catalysis and by that time the field was so saturated that critical papers like [guano-doped graphene](https://pubs.acs.org/ancac3/article-abstract/14/1/21/602927/Will-Any-Crap-We-Put-into-Graphene-Increase-Its?redirectedFrom=fulltext) started to appear, criticizing the overproduction of scientific reports without a clear production of knowledge. 

I was repeating to myself that experimental work did not seem to be the rate determining step for science. Finding, reading, and organizing literature was the real deal, and almost impossible to have a full grasp on it. Review were supposed to help you in that, but often reviews felt more like a self advertasment campaing for the authors rather than a complete perspective of the field. Yet, having more than 10 relevant papers a day coming out, even the best review after few years would turn out outdated.

Compared to those times, frontier large language models have made terrific progress and only during my postdoc they went from inventing literature entries to providing a full perspective on a field. If I have a scientific question, I never open google scholar or web of science, I go directly on claude or chatGPT. Often their analysis will come out too wordy and difficult to read, but if you scroll through the citation list, you get almost all the paper you were supposed to find. So what is the point of a literature review in 2026?

> *Review are there to take responsibility for the words generated by an LLM*

Indeed, while they can perform amazingly on tasks like literature review, LLMs cannot take responsibility if mistakes are made. However, we are still human and if mistakes are made, we need to find a cause. allucinations are not good enough excuse and we need the human in the loop. My bet for the future job market is the same: where knowledge workers will become more and more quality assurance rather than knowledge production.
